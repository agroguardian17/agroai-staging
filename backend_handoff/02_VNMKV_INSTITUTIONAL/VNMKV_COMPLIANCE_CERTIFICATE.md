## 5. Certified — Pesticide registry (with immediate regulatory update)

### 🚨 5.1 CRITICAL REGULATORY UPDATE — Streptocycline Ban

**VNMKV Faculty Verdict: STRICTLY BANNED / BLOCKLISTED — EFFECTIVE 1 JANUARY 2024**

**Regulatory basis:**
- Union Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare, Government of India, Gazette Notification effective 1 January 2024
- CIB&RC restricted-molecule list (post-2024 revision)
- Insecticides Act 1968 (as amended) — prohibition on Streptomycin sulphate 9% + Tetracycline hydrochloride 1% w/w in agriculture

**Scientific/public-health rationale:**
The combination product Streptocycline (marketed as Streptocycline, Paushamycin, Agrimycin) uses Streptomycin, classified by the World Health Organization as a **Critically Important Antimicrobial (CIA)** for human tuberculosis therapy. Multi-year evidence from ICAR Institutes and public-health agencies established that agricultural application, particularly the widespread drench and foliar practice on ginger, pomegranate and citrus crops, was accelerating the emergence of streptomycin-resistant pathogens in soil microbiota with direct spillover risk to human medicine through the food chain. The Ministry acted on the joint recommendation of the CIB&RC, the Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR), and the National Centre for Disease Control.

**Algorithmic mandate for VIRAAI (non-negotiable):**

1. Registry `streptocycline` row must move to `crop_registered = no`, `verification_status = CONFIRMED_BLOCKLIST`
2. `blocklist_reason_if_any` must state: *"BANNED IN AGRICULTURE from 1 January 2024 per Union Ministry of Agriculture Gazette Notification. Streptomycin is a WHO Critically Important Antimicrobial for human medicine; agricultural use accelerates antimicrobial resistance (AMR)."*
3. Any user-input intention to apply Streptocycline shall trigger a critical-violation alert; the system shall not emit any advisory, dose, or PHI for it
4. The system shall pivot such users to the Section 6 biological drench sequence + 5-7 year rotation for bacterial wilt management

VNMKV certifies this ban implementation as a regulatory-compliance requirement independent of consultative agronomy — implementation is mandatory regardless of any operational preference.

## 6. Certified — Integrated Disease Management protocol

### 6.1 Soft Rot Preventive Bio-Drench Sequence (mandatory for Marathwada vertisols)

The following three-step protocol is **institutionally certified** for Pythium and Fusarium complex management in Marathwada heavy vertisols. The 12–15 day buffer period between steps 1 and 3 is **non-negotiable** and represents the most common point of farmer failure.

| Step | Timing | Action | Certified Rationale |
|:---:|---|---|---|
| **1. Chemical cleanse** | DAP 0–15 (immediate post-plant) | Copper Oxychloride 2.5–3.0 g/L OR Metalaxyl-M + Mancozeb 2.5 g/L soil drench | Suppresses over-wintering soil-borne inoculum load |
| **2. Buffer period** | 12–15 days mandatory | No biological input · no chemical input | Chemical residue degradation; prevents acute toxicity to incoming bio-agents |
| **3. Biological colonisation** | DAP 12–30 (post-buffer) | Consortium of *Trichoderma viride/harzianum* + *Pseudomonas fluorescens* at 5–10 g/L soil drench, or as solid substrate with 2.5 kg/ha FYM | Direct mycoparasitism (Trichoderma via chitinase/glucanase); rhizosphere competitive exclusion (Pseudomonas) |

**Certified prohibitions:**
- No tank-mix of biologicals with copper, carbendazim, or mancozeb
- No omission of the 12–15 day buffer under any circumstance
- No substitution of Streptocycline for any step (see §5.1)

### 6.2 Bacterial Wilt — certified epidemiological management

**Ralstonia solanacearum persistence in Marathwada vertisols is 5–7 years, NOT 3 years.**

Per VNMKV Plant Pathology surveillance records (2018–2023), re-infection rates on 3-year-rotation plots were 45–60%, versus 8–15% on 5-year-rotation plots. The 3-year benchmark commonly used in lighter soils **does not apply** to Marathwada heavy vertisols where the bacterium survives in the deep clay profile.

**Certified mandate for VIRAAI:**
- Plot with confirmed bacterial wilt in past **5 years** → REFUSE ginger cultivation (block at D01/D06 pre-plant gate)
- Plot with confirmed wilt 5–7 years ago → Ginger permitted **only** with mandatory §6.1 protocol + intensified scouting + biological drench (§6.3)
- Rule D06-BW-001 trigger must be updated: change `years_since_last_wilt < 3` → `years_since_last_wilt < 5`

### 6.3 Bacterial wilt suppression post-Streptocycline

With Streptocycline banned, chemical eradication is not available. Certified management:
- Pseudomonas fluorescens biological drench (§6.1 step 3)
- 5–7 year rotation as primary prevention
- Cultural: BBF architecture, drainage optimisation, tool sterilisation between plots, farmer-hand hygiene, deliberate isolation of wilt-suspect plots
- Removal + solar sterilisation of infected material; no composting

### 6.4 Rhizome Fly (Mimegralla coeruleifrons) — peak window

**CERTIFIED:** mid-July to late August (Marathwada week 28–week 34).

Peak adult flight and oviposition correlates with continuous monsoon rains and RH > 80% for ≥ 5 consecutive days. Timely planting (§7.2 optimal window closes 7 June) is the single most effective non-chemical mitigation as it ensures crop is at hardened G2 stage during the peak.

### 7.2 Optimal planting window — certified (Kannad Western zone)

**Certified window: 15 May – 7 June.**

**7 June is absolute** — extending to 15 June is NOT viable in Western Marathwada Scarcity zone. Pre-monsoon planting allows rhizome establishment before the July-August rhizome fly peak; delayed planting leaves the crop in soft G0/early-G1 during peak oviposition.

**Certified penalty schedule (VIRAAI D11 U-value factor 13):**

| Planting date | Yield-loss factor `u_i × I_i` | AI recommendation |

| ≤ 7 June | 0 | Normal advisory schedule |

## 8. Certified — Standing Marathwada directives

### 8.1 Broad-Bed-and-Furrow (BBF) architecture — MANDATORY

For any Kannad plot on Marathwada vertisol: certified mandatory geometry —
- Bed height: minimum 15–20 cm
- Furrow spacing: 60 cm between beds
- Main drainage channel: at lowest plot point, outlet-connected outside field

Per VNMKV research records, flat-layout planting on heavy vertisol under monsoon conditions produced 35–50 % yield loss and 3× soft-rot incidence versus BBF. VIRAAI rule D02-LY-001 must be moved from CONDITIONAL to **BLOCKING** status.

### 8.2 Basal Zinc Sulphate — standing recommendation

Certified default: **Zinc Sulphate 25 kg/ha at land preparation** for every Kannad plot. Not diagnostic-triggered — this is a preventive baseline recommendation for the calcareous vertisol context (pH 7.3–8.5, CaCO₃ 3.3–16.5%). Backend must add as default D02/D04 rule for the Western Marathwada zone.

### 8.3 Certified — Marathwada micronutrient priority ranking

| Rank | Deficiency | Certified corrective |
|:---:|---|---|
| **1** | **Zinc (Zn)** — most widespread and severe | Basal ZnSO₄ 25 kg/ha + foliar ZnSO₄ 0.5% at DAP 60 & 90 |
| **2** | **Iron (Fe)** — bicarbonate-induced chlorosis on new leaves | Foliar FeSO₄ 0.5% + citric acid 0.1% at DAP 60 & 90 |
| **3** | **Boron (B)** — cell-wall integrity for rhizome bulking | Foliar Borax 0.2% at DAP 90 |
| **4** | **Manganese (Mn)** — less critical, only if leaf-tissue confirmed | Foliar MnSO₄ 0.5% conditional |
